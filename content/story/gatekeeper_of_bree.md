# The Gatekeeper of Bree

The night was clear, and white stars were shining, when the four hobbits approached the gate.
Hobbits traveling out from the Shire had become a rare event in recent days.
Even those halflings from Buckland, most familiar with the Big Folk out of all their kin, had been taking the road to Bree less and less.

Furthermore, travelers seldom came at night.
Sundown was the unspoken deadline for a wanderer to enter the town, and as such old Harry the gatekeeper wasn't expecting any company.
One could imagine his surprise, then, when he looked up and found four small silhouettes standing at the gate.

He leaped out of his seat and reached for the lantern that hung on the doorknob of his gatehouse.
The candle would need to be replaced soon, but for now it still burned with a yellow light.
Rushing to the window, he craned his neck and peered out.

"What do you want, and where do you come from?" he asked gruffly.
Straight to the point.
Harry was the gatekeeper, after all, and what good was a gatekeeper if he didn't keep tabs on the folk that came into town?

"We are making for the inn here," answered the first hobbit, who stood at the front of the group.
"We are journeying east and cannot go further tonight."

"Hobbits!
Four hobbits!" Harry exclaimed, more so to himself than to anyone listening.
"And what's more, out of the Shire by their talk."
He paused for a moment, eyeing each of them.
The second hobbit, closest to the first, was short and chubby, and despite his unthreatening appearance, Harry felt a hard, guarded gaze from him.
The other two stood behind the first pair, one with a watchful expression and the other aloof, kicking a pebble in the dirt.

Despite his initial surprise, Harry wasn't worried about four hobbits, no matter what time of day they came.
So, he opened the gate, and the hobbits passed through.

"We don't often see Shire-folk on the road at night," he said.
"You'll pardon my wondering what business takes you away east of Bree!
What may your names be, might I ask?"

The first hobbit, turned and spoke.
"Our names and our business are our own, and this does not seem a good place to discuss them," he said.

Harry eyed him.
"Your business is your own, no doubt," he said, "but it's my business to ask questions after nightfall."

"We are hobbits from Buckland," piped up one of the hobbits in the back of the group.
"We have a fancy to travel and to stay at the inn here.
I am Mr. Brandybuck.
Is that enough for you?"
He folded his arms.
"The Bree-folk used to be fair-spoken to travelers, or so I had heard."

Harry sighed in frustration.
"All right, all right!" He said, relenting.
"I meant no offence.
But you'll find maybe that more folk than old Harry at the gate will be asking you questions.
There's queer folk about.
If you go on to *The Pony*, you'll find you're not the only guests."

With that the hobbits bade him good night and road up the hill further into town.
Harry watched them go, until at last they turned the corner and went out of sight.
Then, he turned and sat back down inside the gatehouse.
The hobbits seemed as harmless as the next, but the old gatekeeper wasn't a fool; he could tell they traveled with a secret.

He had meant what he told them: strange folk were traveling on the road these days.
It was a warning to the hobbits, and a reminder to himself to keep a careful watch.
In all his long days watching the West-gate of Bree, nothing more than drunks or the occasional ruffian had given him trouble.
Now, though, there was an unseen malice in the night.
There was a sinister chill in the air, and when the wind blew through the gatehouse, Harry shivered.

The next day passed quickly, and Harry saw to the regular comings and goings of folk, most of them turning north or south along the Greenway.
At one point in the afternoon, Bill Ferny rode in through the west gate, ignoring Harry entirely.
There was a large sack strung to his saddle that clinked with each step of his horse.
Coins.

"Now hold on a minute, Ferny!" Harry called out, standing in the doorway of the gatehouse.
"You came in through this gate yesterday, but I didn't see you leave at any point this morning."

Ferny stayed his horse and turned.
"I went out the south gate this morning, old man."

"And what business are you about that brings such a handsome payment as that?"
Harry indicated to the sack of coins.
He had never liked that Bill Ferny, being so willing to do nefarious nogoods in exchange for payment.

"My business is for me alone, and none of it is reserved for your ears, gatekeeper.
I come and go when I please, and where I please."
He scowled then kicked his horse in motion.

Harry shook his head, but before he could speak, Ferny had stopped his horse again and turned back towards the gatehouse.

"Tell me this!
Did four hobbits come through the gate last night?"

The gatekeeper was surprised to hear this, and he froze for a moment.
He didn't reply before Bill Ferny read his expression and spoke again.

"So they did, then."
He smirked.
"I was very pleased to see them.
They gave quite a show at the inn last night."

Harry furrowed his brows.
"What do you care about four hobbits?"

Bill Ferny had already turned away and had begun riding up the hill.
At the question, he turned his head and called back in response.
"Oh, don't you bother to worry, old man.
But I must thank you for letting them in!"
He laughed rudely and rode on.

Harry glared at the back of Ferny's head as he rode up the hill, then sighed and stepped back into the gatehouse.
Something was amiss, but Harry couldn't guess what.
Four unassuming hobbits show up in the dead of night, and the very next day Bill Ferny is laughing about them like they're the butt of a terrible joke.
That man was always up to something, and it was never something good.

The old gatekeeper sat in the gatehouse and puzzled at these odd events until the afternoon was spent and the evening was waning.
The setting sun brought in the final wave of travelers, most of them heading to the inn.
A sudden chill had taken the night after the sun departed, and Harry had to don his cloak to keep warm.

When the stars winked through the darkening sky, odd sounds began to drift in on the wind.
High-pitched wails, like the distant shrieks of an animal, found Harry's ears and made him shudder.
His gaze was turned inward, puzzling at the four hobbits, when a sound outside the gate snapped him back to awareness.

Galloping horses.
They were still far, but they would be here in a minute or two.
Something stirred him to act.
Perhaps it was Harry's sense of unease from the shrieks on the wind, or perhaps it was his suspicion of Bill Ferny.
Trouble was coming to Bree, and it was nearly here.

The gatekeeper jumped to his feet, grabbed his lantern, and ran to the gate.
With a great heave, he swung the gate shut.
The galloping was getting nearer.

Still not sure what the danger was, he slammed the locking bar down into its slot.
The gate was shut and locked.
His heart thumped along and his mind was racing.

"That damn Ferny did something," Harry muttered.
"Those hobbits are in trouble."
He ran back into the gatehouse where he could peek out beyond the shut gate.
It was too dark to see, but the galloping was nearly upon him.
"No, not just them -- everyone in Bree is in trouble!"

He glanced to the far wall, where a long spear, old and in disrepair, was leaning in the corner.
Harry had received it from the Bree guard, more so as a formality than as a precaution.
He had never used it.
Not once.
But that night, he picked it up.

The galloping slowed down and came to a stop.
Harry ran to the wall and grabbed the spear, then crept up to the window.

Two black horses stood before the shut gate, and atop them sat two riders, entirely clothed in black.
The horses stamped impatiently in the dirt and whinnied madly.
One of the riders noticed him, and turned its cloaked head towards him.
Under the hood, Harry could see nothing but shadow.

"Open the gate," hissed the rider.
Chills shot through Harry like a biting winter wind, and he felt fear.

"Open the gate and let us in." the second rider barked.
A gust of wind threw their tattered black cloaks about them.

For a moment, the gatekeeper was frozen with terror.
But then, he looked down at his hands, and saw the spear that he gripped with white knuckles.
Mastering his fear, he stood and spoke.

"I will not!" Harry said defiantly, though his voice shook.
"Begone!
Don't bring your mischief into my town."

"Open the gate," hissed the first rider, "and your life will be spared."
Both riders were staring through him.
"Deny us entry, and we will end you."

Despite it all, Harry found his courage.
He met their gaze one last time, calling out as bravely as he could.
"I will not open this gate for you, not now, not ever!
Turn away and do not return!"

Then, he ran out of the gatehouse into the inner courtyard, then stood and faced the inside of the gate.
He gripped his spear and stood fast, then called out.
"Guards!
Guards!
There is mischief at the West-gate!
Come quick!"

Harry never found out if the guards heard his cries.
A storm of gallops sounded outside the gate, and it came crashing down, thrown down by a force that was too strong for ordinary men.
The two riders rushed in and came straight towards the old gatekeeper.
He held his spear strong, but it was to no avail.
The last thing he saw was a flash of steel.

The candle ran out in the gatehouse, and the riders tore up the hill towards the inn.
