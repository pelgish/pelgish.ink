# Looking Up



A boy looked up into the night sky.
The night was nearly clear, save for a few puffs of clouds that floated by, occasionally obscuring the stars that were scattered across the sphere.
The moon was nowhere to be found; probably hanging in somebody else's sky.

Earlier, he had extinguished all the lanterns that hung from the roof.
They swung gently from their chains as a gentle breeze passed through.
It was utterly dark.
Any remaining light was trapped within the walls of the house, trying desperately to burst forth into the night.

Out came his mother.
She sat with him on the steps leading up to the front door.
"Here, drink this." she said.
He took the cup of hot tea and sipped at it carefully.
A comforting smell of herbs and roses drifted upward.

There was a chill in the air that night.
Spring was nearly arrived, but something must have delayed it, because the fingers of winter still clung to the land.

She watched her son for a moment, then joined him in his upward gaze.
In and out, their breath rose and fell together for a few quiet moments.
Finally, the boy spoke.

"How far away are they?"
The stars.

"Farther than we can imagine." said his Mother.
"Some say they get farther away each day."
She pondered her own words.

"Where are they going, then?"

She smiled at such an innocent question.
But after a moment's thought, she realized there was no answer she could give him.
"Nobody knows.
They're heading somewhere, and one day they'll get there.
But nobody's quite figured that out yet."

He took another sip of his tea.
More herbs and roses.
If he was confused, or perhaps unsatisfied, he didn't show it.
Instead, he moved onto a new topic.

"Where are we?"

"We're at home, silly."

"No, I mean in space!"

"Why, we're on the planet Earth, of course."

"But where is the planet Earth?"

His mother smiled.
"It's in our solar system, orbiting the sun."

"Then where is our solar system?"

"It's floating along with the other stars in our galaxy."

"The Milky Way?"

"Yes, the Milky Way."

That seemed to satisfy him for a minute.
He shifted to aim his gaze directly above him.
His head moved subtly to the left and right as he scanned the band of stars that formed one of the long arms of the galaxy.
The stunning view stretched from one horizon to the other.
Sure, the cosmos is dark and empty, but in some places, it is stunningly beautiful.

The simple questions of the child drove the mother into profound contemplations.
Thoughts and dreams, hopes and fears, some that cannot be described by human language, passed through her mind.
The ancient questions that have forever occupied humanity hung about them.
She looked down at her son, then back up at the sky.
He took another sip of the tea.

"But," he continued, "where is the Milky Way?"

She knew where this train of thought was headed.
"The universe." She said.
"We are in a place so large that we can't even imagine how big it is."

"Are there other galaxies?" He asked.
She nodded.

"Yes, there are lots of other galaxies.
They are in the universe with us."

"Maybe that's why the stars are running away from us," reasoned the boy.
"If it's really that big, maybe they want to go and see it all."

She smiled.
"Yes, maybe so."
She gently ran her fingers through his hair as he gazed into the night.

"So if lots of stars are in a galaxy, and lots of galaxies are in the universe," he mused, "then are there lots of universes?"

She nodded slowly, not to answer *yes*, but to give her body something to do while her mind tried to work out an answer.
"There might be.
But our universe is the only one we've ever known."

"And what would all those universes be inside of?"

She chuckled.
"You are asking some questions that the smartest people in the world don't have an answer to."

"I think I'd like to figure out an answer.
It seems like an awfully important thing to know."
And just like that, a scientific mind was born.

They sat in silence for a time.
The cup of tea slowly drained as the boy took more sips.
More herbs and roses hung in the air.

"I wish we didn't have to ask these questions," said the boy with disappointment.
"Why can't we just *know* these things?"

Again, his mother didn't have an answer for such a simple question.
She thought for a moment, then at last spoke.
"I think it might be our job to answer these questions." she ruminated.
"Perhaps we were put here to figure them out.
Besides, life wouldn't be very interesting if we already had all the answers."

Of course, like children do, he took his mother's answer as the undisputed truth.
"Then if it's our job to answer these questions, who gave it to us?"

"I suppose we gave ourselves the job.
People have always been curious about things."

"But who put us here?
Did somebody make the universe just for us?"

Silence.
His mother didn't have an answer.
She stared out into the void, the dark spaces between the stars.
Subconsciously, her eyes were searching for an answer, way out there, past the most distance galaxy, at the edges of space and time.
How *did* we really get here?

Enough time passed in silence that the boy turned to watch his mother.
He was confused by her lack of a response, but as he watched her, he came to the slow understanding that this was a question not even his mom could answer.

Finally, she fell out of her reverie, her focus retreating from the cosmic cliffs back to the front porch.
She smiled at him.
"I guess that's another question you'll have to find an answer to.
When you do, you'll change our species forever."

The boy smiled back, gladly accepting the charge.
He was going to figure this out.

The cup of tea was empty.
The night deepened, and sleep called to them.
At last, his mother rose, and patted him on the head.
"Come on, it's late.
Let's get some sleep."

They stepped inside and closed the door.
Tomorrow, the sun would rise; the very same that had risen for billions of years on the planet Earth.
Humanity would awaken to the same cosmos, a place as beautiful and unexplainable as it had ever been.
But a change, no matter how small, yields an altered universe.
Perhaps he really will figure it out.
