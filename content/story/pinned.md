# Pinned





By mid-afternoon, Jim and Eve's new house was stuffed with neighbors.
Paper plates were piled high in the kitchen trash, and the coolers in the backyard were frequently emptying under the heat of the summer sun.
Eve was deep in a conversation when Jim excused himself.

After a few hours, his overextended social batteries were spent.
Slowly, he made his way through the backyard, into the kitchen, shaking hands and offering drinks, finally reaching his sanctuary at the front of the house.

But he wasn't alone; a man was there, and so Jim reassumed his host's smile and approached.
The man turned to greet him.

"Hey, welcome to the neighborhood!" said the man.
"You and your wife have really put on quite the housewarming party.
And so soon after moving in!"

"Ha! Thanks," said Jim, "we just unpacked the last box two days ago.
I'm Jim."
He extended his hand.

"Marcus!" He shook it vigorously.
"I live three houses down, at twenty-three oh six."
Another man approached the two.

"Some room you've got here!" said the new man, studying the walls.
Corkboards and frames were packed full of maps, photographs, foreign currencies, and other trinkets from the couple's travels.
At the center of it all was Jim's globe, a two-hundred-year-old antique, obtained from a market in the Spanish countryside on his very first international trip with Eve.
The 'map room,' as the couple lovingly called it, was Jim's favorite room in the house: a monument to their adventures across the Earth.
"I'm Clyde."

The three men exchanged greetings, and Jim ducked into the kitchen to fetch them something to drink.
When he came back, Marcus and Clyde were examining the globe.
Metal pins with red heads dotted the surface, marking all the places Jim and Eve had visited together.

Clyde pointed at the globe.
"Is this thing telling me that you've been to all seven continents?"

"Yep!
Now we're looking for the eighth."
Jim grinned and took a sip as the two men laughed.
He was in the map room, and the conversation had shifted to his favorite topic.

Marcus reached down and spun the globe.
"Africa, wow," he marveled.
"I've always wanted to see the elephants."

"Let me tell you," Jim started, "they're *massive*.
We were there last winter on a safari.
Beautiful creatures, but whatever you do, don't piss them off!"

"Y'know," Clyde tipped his bottle towards a photograph of the Great Pyramids, "I once read a book about Egypt and the pyramids.
I've heard they used to--"

Jim had already spun the globe again and pressed his finger into a pin over Cairo.
"Absolutely breathtaking.
We were there in 2021.
Did you know they used to be covered in white limestone, and capped with gold?"

Clyde blinked.
A twinge of disappointment passed over his expression, but only Marcus noticed.
"Yeah.
I read that once -- such interesting history."

"I've never been out of the country, but a few years ago, we took the kids to Colorado," Marcus began.
"My God -- the Rockies are incredible.
I've never seen that much snow in my life.
I'd like to go back someday."

The globe was spinning as soon as Marcus said "Colorado."
Jim's fingers drifted to the cluster of pins jammed into the Rocky Mountains.
Marcus' eyes fell on them as he spoke, and some of the energy in his voice faded.
He exchanged a quick glance with Clyde.

"Well," Marcus began again, "every trip taken claims something in return." He shrugged.
"I'm always glad to be back in my neck of the woods."

Clyde raised his bottle in agreement.
"Amen to that.
There's not a day that goes by where I don't think about sitting on some beach in the Caribbean.
But once I'm out there, about three or four days in, I always find myself daydreaming of home."
They nodded to each other.

"So, what else do you like to do?" asked Marcus.

Jim paused, held by introspection.
There was nothing else.
He rubbed his neck.
"Well, not much else, honestly."

"Play any sports?"

He shook his head.

"*Watch* any sports?"

"I've never really been much of a sports guy."

"How about movies?
Or books?"
Clyde probed.

Jim shrugged.
He couldn't remember the last time he sat down and watched a movie.

"For a while, in college, I really dove into board games," offered Marcus.
"What'd you do before you started traveling?"

Silence.
Marcus and Clyde watched him expectantly, leaving a wide gap for Jim to fill.
He looked around the map room, searching for an answer.
But there was nothing else.
This room was the evidence.

"Would you say your travels are part of your identity?" asked Marcus.

Jim cocked his head.
"What do you mean?"

"Your identity.
Does it change when you visit these places?"
He pulled a pin out of the globe and examined it.

Something jerked within him as Marcus removed the pin.
Jim watched him hold it up to the light.
He wasn't sure how to respond.

After a moment, he answered.
"I... yes, I suppose they are a part of me.
It's my biggest passion in life."
What else was there?

Marcus nodded slowly, his expression caught between curiosity and assessment.
Then he smiled.
"I can believe it.
It really is an inspiring thing to do."
He put the pin back in place.

"Well, hey -- you'll never run out of stories!"
Clyde grinned.



Jim reached out to spin the globe, then hesitated.
Instead, he looked at the two men and forced a smile.
"It's been great to meet you.
I had better go check on Eve."

They tipped their bottles and Jim stepped away.
Before entering the kitchen, he looked back at the map room.
For once, the globe was still.
Everything else was spinning.
